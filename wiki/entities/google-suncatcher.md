# Google Suncatcher

## Basic Information

Google (Alphabet) **Project Suncatcher** — a research "moonshot" to scale machine-learning compute in space using fleets of solar-powered satellites carrying **TPUs** linked by **free-space optics**. Formally announced **2025-11-04** with a published system-design pre-print and a Google Research blog post; partner **Planet** builds and operates the prototype hardware.

> **Flight inflection (2026-10) — now FLOWN, online, and returning its first in-orbit result.** Suncatcher crossed from pre-print to *flown validation*. Google's first satellite — a refrigerator-sized single-node validator it calls **MVP**, carrying **four TPUs** (prior reporting said Trillium-generation; Google has **not publicly confirmed the generation** aboard) fed by a **~1 kW** solar array and **built by Planet Labs** — **launched 2026-10-01 on a SpaceX Falcon 9 (Transporter-18 rideshare) from Vandenberg SFB** and Google confirmed it came online operating as expected. On **2026-10-31 Google reported the first AI response ever generated by a data-center-class TPU in orbit**: the satellite ran **Gemma 4** in LEO and beamed the answer — a haiku — down to a ground station at **Svalbard**. A concrete **layer-down power constraint** surfaced with the result: on its ~1 kW budget the validator can run **only one of its four TPUs for ~20 minutes at a time**, so MVP is a *commissioning / radiation-&-thermal-stress* campaign, not a sustained-compute demonstrator. The stated goal over the coming weeks-to-months is **in-orbit TID + thermal telemetry** on the TPUs; beyond the "it works / first-answer" milestone **no quantitative on-orbit radiation or thermal data has yet been published**. This is the demo→flight step the earlier "early-2027" note anticipated; the **paired two-satellite mission that tests the high-bandwidth free-space laser links** (the technology any real 81-sat cluster depends on) stays in **2027**. Corroborated across ≥2 independently-named outlets ([Google on X, 2026-10-01](https://x.com/Google/status/2105803583648100611), [GCN](https://gcn.com/google-project-suncatcher-ai-satellite/22842/), [New Space Economy 2026-10-31](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/10/31/googles-project-suncatcher-generates-an-ai-response-in-orbit/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-ai-chip-in-orbit-answers-its-first-prompt-with-a-haiku/)); WebFetch proxy-blocked, read via search snippets.

> Status update (2026-06-03): the earlier "limited publicly disclosed detail" caveat is now superseded — Google has published a technical pre-print and confirmed a Planet-built prototype mission. SatNews (2026-01) additionally reports Google has begun addressing orbital-debris risk for the constellation.

## Core Characteristics

| Element | Description |
|------|------|
| Computing core | Google in-house **Trillium-generation TPU**; survived particle-accelerator radiation testing simulating LEO levels without damage to the logic |
| Architecture | Pre-print sketches an **81-satellite cluster** flying in formation within a **~1 km radius**, with free-space optical inter-satellite links; a **bench-scale free-space optical link at 1.6 Tbps total bandwidth** is already demonstrated in the lab (the US *bandwidth-ceiling* datum) |
| Orbit | **Sun-synchronous orbit ≈650 km** — chosen for near-constant solar exposure (Google estimates panels up to **~8× more productive** than on Earth) |

| Timeline | **MVP single-node validator (4 TPUs, ~1 kW) launched 2026-10-01** on Falcon 9 Transporter-18 (Vandenberg); came online operating as expected; **first in-orbit AI answer (Gemma 4, to Svalbard) 2026-10-31**; **paired two-satellite laser-ISL demo → 2027**; 81-sat cluster scale and final design explicitly flagged as subject to change |
| Strategic intent | Leverage cheap, abundant solar power to scale AI compute beyond terrestrial grid/cooling limits |

## Radiation Result — The Memory, Not the Logic, Is the Weak Point

The widely-quoted "Trillium TPU survived LEO-simulated radiation" headline has an important layer-down qualifier, now with **quantified beam figures** from Google's pre-print (verified 2026-07): the chips were exposed to a **67 MeV proton beam** (reported as a **~5-year-equivalent mission dose** administered at the UC Davis cyclotron), and Google reports **no TID-attributable hard failures up to the maximum tested 15 krad(Si) on a single chip** — so the TPU **logic** is "surprisingly radiation-hard." These remain *ground-accelerator* figures; the **2026-10 MVP flight** (flight-inflection callout above) is precisely the mission meant to replace them with real orbital TID/thermal data on the exact HBM subsystem flagged below — though as of 2026-11 no such on-orbit telemetry has been published yet. The catch is the memory: the **High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) subsystem was the most TID-sensitive part**, showing first irregularities at only **~2 krad(Si)** — yet even that is **~3× the expected shielded 5-year mission dose of 750 rad(Si)**, a rate Google judged "**likely acceptable for inference**" (but, by implication, *not* obviously acceptable for long-duration training). Source: [Google Research, "Exploring a space-based, scalable AI infrastructure system design" (2025-11)](https://research.google/blog/exploring-a-space-based-scalable-ai-infrastructure-system-design/). This is the same failure mode the radiation cluster already flags — [[concepts/cots-gpu-radiation-risk]] notes that GPU/TPU ECC covers HBM but offers no TID protection. It reinforces the cluster's central caveat (carried on [[synthesis/orbital-data-center-six-region]]): an accelerator surviving an *accelerator beam test for hours* is not the same as a *memory stack surviving years* of cumulative dose, and it nudges Suncatcher toward an inference-tier role rather than orbital training.

## Strategic Significance

Google Suncatcher is the clearest signal that **hyperscalers**, not just startups, are entering [[concepts/orbital-data-center]]. Where [[entities/starcloud]] proved a COTS NVIDIA H100 works in orbit and [[entities/axiom-space]] proved the relay-networked edge node, Suncatcher diversifies the compute layer onto **proprietary TPUs** and bets on **formation flight + free-space optics** rather than a single large platform — making optical ISL ([[concepts/leo-value-chain]] midstream A) the load-bearing technology. With the **MVP validator now flown (2026-10-01) and operating** — and having returned the **first AI answer from a data-center-class TPU in orbit (Gemma 4, 2026-10-31)** — Suncatcher is the **third US actor to fly a TPU/GPU-class accelerator in orbit** (after [[entities/starcloud]]'s H100 and [[entities/axiom-space]]'s relay nodes) — but note the split in what has been *proven*: MVP validates the *TPU-runs-and-survives* half of the bet (and even that only at ~1 TPU for ~20 min at a time, pending radiation/thermal telemetry), while the *high-bandwidth-optical* half (the 1.6 Tbps bench link that a real cluster's formation flight depends on) still awaits the 2027 paired demo. So even after MVP flies, Suncatcher's optical claim stays **bench-not-flown** versus [[entities/ada-space]]'s **multi-day flown** laser-ISL endurance (the flown-vs-bench split in cross-cutting read 2 of the six-region synthesis). In the six-region map ([[synthesis/orbital-data-center-six-region]]) it is the US hyperscaler entry, contrasting with China's state-scaled [[entities/ada-space]] and Europe's study-stage ASCEND. On the optical-ISL front specifically, Suncatcher's **1.6 Tbps bench link** is the US *bandwidth-ceiling* proof point — but it is still a *lab* result, whereas [[entities/ada-space]] has flown a **multi-day (8-day) laser ISL in orbit**: the US leads on bench bandwidth, China on demonstrated-in-orbit endurance (the flown-vs-bench split in cross-cutting read 2 of the six-region synthesis).
